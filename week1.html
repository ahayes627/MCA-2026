Week 1: Basics of Music Data

Task 1: Identify a theme

I've chosen to base my dataset around the American multi-hyphenate - singer, songwriter, composer, pianist, and civil rights activist - Nina Simone. Although referred to as the 'High Priestess of Soul,' Simone's music is difficult to pin down to just one style, switching between genres like jazz, blues, and classical. The expansiveness and diversity of her catalogue is both why I'm a fan of Nina Simone's work and why I picked her as the theme for the dataset, as I am curious about analysing the differences between different genres and instruments in both notated and audio music.

Task 2: Reflection

Consider your own experience with music. What do you think are some challenges to working with music and music-related data? These could be related to issues like access, curation, distribution, etc. How does your selected theme display some of these challenges?

Through my own experience with music and in terms of my selected theme, I can identify several challenges to working with music and music-related data, including issues around access, curation, and distribution.

Access Challenges:

  • Personal experience: I have often had issues accessing music and music data like storage issues and format/technological obsolescence. For example, I have not been able to access the data that was stored on my 3rd generation iPod touch, as the battery was uncharged for so long that it locked me out for the next few decades. Even if this was not an issue, many of the apps are no longer supported with the current iOS updates. Other access issues are the high costs of music streaming services. As a student, I have to turn to free music libraries, where the selection of music is smaller.
    •  
  • Nina Simone: With most of her artistic activity being in the1960s and 1970s, before the modern iteration of the internet, many of the recording devices and tools used to record Nina Simone’s performances may be degrading with time or are not compatible with modern technologies. For instance, if not properly stored and preserved, vinyl records can be damaged by dust and scratching.

Curation Challenges:

  • Personal experience: Although free music libraries offer an affordable option for listening to music, I have found that their catalogues are less extensive than paid streaming services. Moreover, between different digital music libraries and streaming services, there are large gaps in the types and quantity of metadata collected, even for the same piece of music. This makes it difficult to describe and understand music data, as the contextual information about a piece of music might not be the same on YouTube and Spotify.
    •  
  • Nina Simone: As Nina Simone covered many songs and many artists have covered hers, it makes it difficult to create the proper metadata for the music, especially in terms of who to credit as the composer and author. Also, as a popular artist, many different copies of each performance may exist, making it difficult to determine which is authentic.

Distribution Challenges:

  • Personal experience: As a music user, my understanding of distribution issues comes from using different platforms that distribute music. Often, the content that I want to consume affects which platform I will listen on. Sometimes, artists are not available to stream on every platform due to distribution rights or ethical considerations. For example, Joanna Newsom’s music is not available on Spotify due to her issues with how the company pays artists. Also, to dodge copyright, some smaller DJs will distribute their remixes on YouTube or SoundCloud, though they could still face removal.
    •  
  • Nina Simone: Having passed away in 2003, Nina Simone’s music is still protected by copyright for decades to come. However, as a prolific live performer, ownership over live performances is protected by different rights than studio recordings. Furthermore, there have been legal battles between Sony Music and Nina Simone’s estate over who has authority over her music. If the people who claim to have the right to distribute Nina Simone’s music want to be the only ones doing so, then they need to be well-versed in legal standards and rights.
